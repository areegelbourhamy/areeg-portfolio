Four parts of the way I think and work.

A medical student who spends her time arguing with data.

It sounds like an odd combination. One day, I'm studying the anatomy of the human body. Another, I'm cleaning a dataset, building a dashboard, or trying to understand why a number refuses to make sense. Somewhere in between, I'm reading research papers, building projects, leading teams, and wondering whether an idea that exists on paper can actually work in the real world.

I like it that way. I've never been particularly interested in staying inside one lane. My starting point is medicine. It gives me the questions — the complicated ones, the human ones, the ones where there is rarely a perfect answer. Then comes data. I work with Excel, Power BI, SQL, Python, and analytics to turn scattered information into something that can be understood.

Research adds another layer. It teaches me to question what I think I know, look for evidence, and turn curiosity into something testable. Along the way, I've found myself moving between classrooms, datasets, research questions, competitions, projects, and student initiatives — constantly picking up something from one world and carrying it into another.

And maybe that's what I'm actually interested in: not medicine alone, not data alone, not research alone, but the space where they collide.

I'm still figuring out where this combination will take me. But I'm fairly certain it won't be boring.
